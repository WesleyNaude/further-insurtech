# Evidence

Every external claim in the pitch, with its source and its weakness. Organised by
the question an investor is likely to ask.

**Read the caveats.** Several of these numbers are widely repeated and weakly
sourced. Where that is true I have said so rather than letting it pass, because
being caught on a soft number costs more than the number was worth.

---

## 1. "The market is huge and under-served"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Uninsured vehicles in South Africa | **65%**, about **8.74 million** vehicles | South African Insurance Association, cited Sept 2023 |
| Reason given | Affordability; **22%** of respondents said they could not afford it (Gen X 31%, Boomers 27%) | Insurer survey coverage, 2026 |
| Typical comprehensive premium | **R800 to R1,400** a month | Market comparison, 2026 |
| SA motor insurance market | **$9.19bn by 2033**, CAGR 8.30% | Spherical Insights |
| Motor as a share of non-life premiums | **41.2%** (2023) | SA short-term insurance market analyses |

**Caveat.** The 65% figure is quoted everywhere and traces back to industry
bodies rather than a published national audit; older AA statements said 70% of
12 million vehicles. Treat it as "roughly two thirds", which is what the app
says. Do not defend a decimal place.

---

## 2. "People will install a tracking app and change behaviour for money"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel cash back paid by one SA insurer | **R1 billion** | Discovery Insure |
| Current reward ceiling | Up to **R1,500 a month** at bp or Shell, raised from R800 in 2026 | Discovery Vitality Drive 3.0 |
| Behavioural effect claimed | Most engaged drivers have **39% fewer** and **44% less severe** accidents | Discovery Insure |

**Why this matters.** It settles the adoption question before we ask it.
South Africans at scale already install a telematics app, tolerate background
tracking, and respond to cash. We are not testing that hypothesis.

**Caveat.** The 39%/44% figures are the insurer's own and are not
selection-adjusted: safer drivers are likelier to engage in the first place.
Quote them as evidence of engagement, not as proof of causation.

## 3. "Low mileage is real underwriting, not a gimmick"

| Product | Claim |
|---|---|
| **King Price Chilli** | Pay-per-kilometre. From about **R299/month** under 100 km, **50–70%** saving at ~300 km/month |
| **Naked Chilli** | Pay-per-kilometre, up to **70%** for low mileage |
| **Pineapple "Drive Less, Get Blessed"** | Up to **30%** cash back |

**This is the slide that kills a naive pitch.** Do not claim low-mileage pricing
is an unserved gap. It is a category with three named competitors. Our claim is
narrower and survives: all three price a car that is *already insured*, belonging
to a customer who already exists and has already consented to tracking. None of
them can reach a person before there is a policy.

## 4. "There is capital for this, with this mandate"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Naked Series B2, Jan 2025 | **R700 million (~$38m)**, largest insurtech round in Africa | Multiple, Jan 2025 |
| Investors | Yellowwoods, **IFC**, Germany's **DEG**, **BlueOrchard** | Same |
| Stated purpose | AI capability, product expansion, and **accessibility for underserved populations** | Same |

Three of those four are development finance or impact investors. The uninsured
majority is their mandate, not a nice-to-have.

**The bear case an investor will raise.** If Naked has $38m and the same mandate,
why not build it? The only durable answer is portability: a credential that works
across insurers is worth more to a consumer than one that locks them in, and no
incumbent ships the product that lets its customers shop.

## 5. "Multimodal travel is normal here, not aspirational"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Workers using private transport as main mode | **43.5%** | StatsSA National Household Travel Survey |
| Workers using public transport | **35.0%** | Same |
| Workers whose main mode is a taxi | **26.5%** (~3.7m people) | Same |
| Individuals using taxis | **10.7 million** | NHTS 2020 |
| Households using public transport who use taxis | **69%** | Same |

**The number we do not have, and need.** None of this tells us how many
*car-owning* households also regularly use public transport. That overlap is our
entire target segment, and it is the single most valuable thing to establish in a
pilot. Say so before someone else points it out.

## 6. "Driving costs more than the fare"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol assumption, 2026 | ~**R27.20/litre** | AA 2026 projections |
| AA / SARS all-in reimbursement | **R4.95 to R6.00 per km** depending on engine | AA, SARS 2026/27 |
| Figure used in the app | **R2.85 per km** | Running cost only |

The app deliberately uses the lower figure, covering fuel, tyres and servicing and
excluding depreciation, licensing and insurance. The comparison therefore
**understates** the case for not driving, which is the right direction to be wrong
in.

## 7. "Incentives change behaviour, but bounties do not last"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dutch **Spitsmijden** peak-avoidance trials | €2–7 per day; peak trips by participants fell about **60%** | Spitsmijden, 2006 onward |
| Reversion | Participants **returned to the peak when payments stopped** | Same |
| Bologna **Bella Mossa** | 15,000 participants, 895,000 sustainable journeys, ~750 t CO2; ~**80%** of journeys replaced a car trip | BetterPoints / Interreg |
| Year two | Active users fell to **10,000** | Same |

**How to use this.** It is the strongest argument for our structure, not against
it. A bounty buys behaviour only while you pay. A premium reduction is a
repricing, so it persists exactly as long as the behaviour does, with no cliff.
The Bella Mossa year-two drop is the fate of every points scheme.

## 8. "Why we killed the carbon model"

| Claim | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| SA carbon tax rate, 2026 | **R308/tonne**; R640 above carbon budget | Treasury, Phase 2 (2026–2030) |
| Carbon fuel levy | Increased 1 April 2026; Treasury has **proposed separating** it from the general fuel levy | Taxation Laws Amendment |
| Ring-fencing | South Africa runs a **single revenue pot**; carbon tax revenue is **not** ring-fenced | SAEEC |
| Value of one commuter's annual carbon saving | **~R300** (~€81 at EU prices) | Derived from the above |
| EU ETS2 | **Delayed to 2028** | EU Climate Law deal, Nov/Dec 2025 |
| EU Social Climate Fund | **€65bn**, live from 2026, plans run 2026–2032 | European Commission |

R300 a year cannot fund a company, a reward, or a behaviour change. That single
number is why the pivot happened, and it is the cleanest way to explain it.

## What I would verify before pitching

1. **The overlap in section 5.** How many car-owning households regularly use
   public transport? This is the market size and we are inferring it.
2. **A real premium quote** for the persona, rather than a market range.
3. **Whether an insurer will actually pay** for a pre-policy exposure profile, and
   what they would pay per qualified lead. Everything commercial rests on this.
4. **The FAIS line.** Selling an underwriting input is fine; quoting or arranging
   cover is a licence.
