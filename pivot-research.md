# Carbon rewards pivot: research and recommendation
Date: 18 September 2026

## 1. What the brief actually is

The PDF is not the original brief. It is a post-mortem pivot memo containing two
unrelated concepts, ending with "Decide which one we're pitching before Friday."
It has already absorbed the lesson from the failed research: on page 2 it states
plainly that a commuter's annual carbon saving is worth about EUR 81, so "this does
not work as a carbon business, it works as a government payments business."

That sentence is the whole pivot. Everything below tests whether it survives contact
with 2026 reality.

## 2. Why the original model failed (confirming the team's finding)

The original app assumed a large pool of corporate carbon money that could be
redistributed to individuals for low-carbon behaviour. It does not exist in a form
anyone can draw from:

- Corporate carbon compliance money is a tax liability paid to a treasury, not a
  grant fund. In South Africa it enters the single national revenue pot; SAEEC
  confirms revenue is explicitly not ring-fenced.
- Voluntary carbon credits require additionality, permanence and audit. An individual
  bus trip fails additionality (the bus ran anyway) and is far too small to audit.
- Unit economics are fatal in both directions: ~0.97 t/year per switched commuter is
  ~EUR 81 at EUR 84/tonne, or ~R300 at R308/tonne. Retail carbon in SA trades around
  R200 per tonne. You cannot fund a behaviour-change reward out of that margin.

Conclusion: correct to kill it. Do not attempt a variant that still monetises the
tonne. The tonne is not the product.

## 3. Verified state of play, September 2026

### EU (Idea 1's premise)
- ETS2 has been **delayed to 2028** (agreed November/December 2025 in the Climate Law
  deal). The brief already reflects this.
- The Social Climate Fund is **live from 2026** and sized at EUR 65bn (EUR 86.7bn with
  national co-financing). National Social Climate Plans run 2026-2032; several member
  states are late submitting.
- The Commission issued implementation guidance in October 2025 and explicitly
  recommends **voucher programmes for public transport and on-demand zero-emission
  services**. That is the brief's product, named in a Commission recommendation.
- Disbursement is milestone-and-target based, and member states are told to reuse
  existing Cohesion/RRF delivery structures. This is the single biggest risk to the
  brief: the default is an incumbent social-payments agency, not a new app.
- France's Forfait Mobilites Durables already runs on dematerialised mobility vouchers
  issued by specialist providers. So the "who delivers it" slot is not empty; there are
  incumbents (Edenred-style voucher issuers) who will bid for SCF work.

### South Africa (Idea 1's second market)
- Carbon tax Phase 2 runs 2026-2030; rate R308/t in 2026, R640/t above carbon budget.
- Carbon fuel levy increased 1 April 2026, and Treasury has formally proposed
  **separating the carbon fuel levy from the general fuel levy** via a new Part 5C of
  Schedule 1 to the Customs and Excise Act, once SARS system upgrades complete. The
  brief is right that this is the precondition for ring-fencing. It is not a commitment
  to ring-fence, and SA runs a one-pot system by doctrine.
- **The taxi industry is digitising right now, without us.** SANTACO piloted cashless
  in the Eastern Cape from March 2026; CODETA went live in Khayelitsha, Killarney and
  Mfuleni on 1 June 2026 with reloadable smart cards and the SAPay app, backed by
  Accion and the Mastercard Center for Inclusive Growth. CODETA already applies R10
  concessionary discounts for scholars, pensioners and clinic trips.

That last point is the most important research finding in this document. It
simultaneously destroys two of the brief's four "hard parts" (cash-only industry,
no rails) and destroys the assumption that we would build the rails. The rails are
being laid by people with more legitimacy than us. The concession logic is already
live on them.

### Idea 2 (flight reward)
- The EU Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive bans offsetting-based
  environmental claims; the Commission and CPC network forced **21 airlines** in
  November 2025 to withdraw or rewrite exactly the claim Idea 2 is built to sell.
- So the airline's compensation is a marketing story it is now legally restricted from
  telling in the EU, and increasingly nervous about telling elsewhere.
- The brief's own closing paragraph concedes the user cannot afford the intervention:
  R8,000 to R48,000 and property control, against a stated user who rents a room.
- The landlord-financed variant is genuinely more interesting but it is a completely
  different company: property-tech energy financing, not a consumer climate app.

Recommendation: **park Idea 2.** It has a defensible mechanic and an indefensible
buyer and user. Keep the landlord-financing insight in a drawer.

## 4. The pivot that survives

Stop selling carbon. Stop selling behaviour change. Sell **eligibility and proof**.

Every transport subsidy that exists or is about to exist has the same unsolved problem:
the money is committed, the recipients are known in aggregate and unknown individually,
and the payer needs defensible evidence that the trip happened and the right person
took it. The EU calls this milestone verification. SANTACO calls it the concession
discount. Treasury calls it targeting.

The product is a **transport entitlement layer**: it establishes who qualifies, proves
the journey against a corridor, and settles the subsidy to whichever rail the commuter
already uses. Revenue is a fee per verified claim or a delivery contract. Never a cut
of the subsidy. The brief already states this correctly.

Three concrete framings, in order of how quickly they can be tested:

**A. Concession-verification layer on SA taxi rails (fastest to prove)**
Partner with SAPay/CODETA rather than compete. They have live card infrastructure and
already run manual concession discounts. We provide eligibility verification (scholar,
pensioner, jobseeker) and a reconciliation record a funder can audit. Payer: initially
a provincial transport department or a donor, not Treasury. Beachhead is one corridor
in Khayelitsha, not a national scheme.

**B. SCF delivery and verification for one small member state (highest ceiling)**
Sell to a member state that is late on its Social Climate Plan and has no domestic
voucher incumbent. Product is the mobility voucher issuance and milestone-evidence
pack. Competition is Edenred-class incumbents and national social agencies, so the
wedge has to be the verification and reporting burden they do not want, not the wallet.

**C. Employer-side sustainable commuting allowance (only self-funding option)**
FMD in France is a live, capped, employer-paid entitlement (EUR 600/year private
sector) with mandatory proof of eligible journeys. The payer is a corporate with a
compliance headache and a budget today. No policy dependency, no 2028 wait. Lowest
ceiling, lowest romance, only one with revenue before 2028.

## 5. What the brief still gets wrong

- **Timing.** ETS2 at 2028 means the EU revenue story is at least two budget cycles
  away. Anything that needs ETS2 money to exist is a 2029 business. Plan accordingly
  or pick a payer who pays now.
- **"Nobody has built the thing that gives it out."** Not true. Voucher issuers,
  social payment agencies and Cohesion/RRF delivery bodies exist and will be reused by
  default. The gap is verification and evidence, not distribution.
- **The SA arithmetic (R5bn / R2.50 per trip / 8.5m unemployed) is a slide, not a
  plan.** It assumes a ring-fence that Treasury has not agreed to. Use it to size the
  prize, never as the business case.
- **Taxi fare capture.** The brief's fear that associations raise fares to absorb a
  visible subsidy is the correct risk and is not solved by making the subsidy invisible.
  It is solved by the association being a partner with upside, which the cashless
  rollout now makes possible.
- **E-money.** Holding funds makes us a regulated issuer. Both A and B should settle
  onto an existing wallet and avoid holding balances entirely.

## 6. Proposed next steps before any app work

1. Pick the payer, not the product. Write one sentence naming who signs the cheque in
   the first 12 months. Everything else follows.
2. Two expert conversations: one with a SAPay/CODETA/SANTACO contact, one with a
   member-state Social Climate Plan lead or a Commission DG EMPL contact.
3. Desk check on the incumbent voucher issuers in the target member state. If there is
   an entrenched one, framing B dies and we go A or C.
4. Size the fee, not the fund. Per-verified-claim pricing against a realistic claim
   volume for one corridor or one employer.
5. Only then: service blueprint, and the app as the thin client on top of it.
